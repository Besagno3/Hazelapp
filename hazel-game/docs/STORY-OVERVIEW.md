# Hazel Quest — Story Overview by Act

As of 2026-10-16. The overarching message, each act's message and beats, and
the hooks that keep kids playing. Reviewed with the owner; the living copy is
the claude.ai doc "Hazel — Story Bible by Act". Detail lives in `STORY.md`
(Act I) and `STORY-4X.md` (Acts II–IV); in-game text in `src/content/`.

## How to review this

This doc covers Hazel Quest's story from the first quiz round to the name finale, as it's written in the game today (`story.ts`, `STORY.md`, `STORY-4X.md`). Read each act's message and beats and confirm, change or comment.

- **Audience:** kids aged 6–18 (question difficulty starts from each child's age and moves with how well they do).
- **Shape:** a short warm-up, then four acts. Act I is open order. Acts II–IV follow one after another.
- **Not covered:** battle numbers, economy tuning, and technical design.

## The overarching message

**Curiosity is a kind of courage. Asking questions, and remembering what matters, is how you bring light back to the world.**

The whole game is built so that learning *is* the magic. Every attack, spell and block in battle runs on a right answer. The villain is not evil. It's what's left when people stop asking hard questions and forget who taught them.

How the message grows across the acts:

| Act | What the child learns about curiosity |
| --- | --- |
| I — The Four Crystals | Your answers matter. Every brave answer gives light back to the world. |
| II — The Crystal of Memory | Forgetting is not the same as losing. What's forgotten can be found again. |
| III — The Starfall Sea | Keep telling your stories out loud. People who share them never forget. |
| IV — The Name | Remembering someone is the kindest power there is. You win with a name, not by hitting harder. |

**Rules every line follows** (from `STORY.md`):

- Wonder, never fear. Fiends are grumpy hoarders, not horrors.
- Mistakes are gentle. Nobody mocks a wrong answer, not even a villain.
- Learning is heroic, never homework. "Brave answer" and "bright idea", never "test" or "grade".
- Every villager gets one joke.

## The story, act by act

### Warm-up — the Training Grounds

**Story message:** you can do this.

A few short quiz rounds on any of the ten topics. There's no story yet on purpose, so kids reach the fun fast. Pass three rounds and the world opens. Then they pick a hero: Valor the lion, Bastion the turtle, Talon the eagle, Kira the fox or Selene the moon-cat.

### Act I — The Four Crystals

**Story message:** your answers matter. Bravery and bright answers bring the light back.

**The setup.** Lumina runs on four Crystals of Knowing: Numbers, Nature, Gears and Wonder. A fog of Forgetting rolled in. Four Fiends drank the crystal light and hid in the far corners of the world. On the morning the fog reached your village, you found the one thing it couldn't touch: **the last dragon egg of Lumina**.

**Key beats**

1. **Opening** (7 storybook panels). The world, the fog, the Fiends, the egg, home, the dark Crystal Spire, and the call to adventure.
2. **Ember hatches** after the first battle won. The baby dragon follows you everywhere and grows with every crystal.
3. **Leaving home.** The land of Dawnreach opens up. A great ring of fog hides the Spire.
4. **Four crystal lands, in any order.** Numbria (math) is always open. The other three are locked behind keys from warden bosses in the Whispering Woods, the Clockwork Depths and Starfall Coast. Each land has a gate, a villager's quest, a Sage who teaches a spell, and a Fiend.
5. **Each crystal restored** lifts a bank of fog on the map, grows Ember, and ends on a dark omen. A voice on the Spire says the Fiends were "only my hands."
6. **The Crystal Spire.** Five spooky floors of riddles, candles that go out on wrong answers, and **Umbra, the Forgotten One**, at the top.
7. **Homecoming.** Umbra unravels, Ember roars full-grown, the village hangs every lantern, and the hero sleeps at the Sleepy Sheep Inn.

**Villain voice:** grumpy, theatrical, oddly lonely. On defeat each Fiend admits the lesson ("your answers… counted").

### Act II — The Crystal of Memory

**Story message:** forgetting isn't losing. What's forgotten can be found again.

**The setup.** You wake to sunshine. The fog is gone and Lumina starts remembering things it forgot. Grandmother Wick: "We didn't lose those places, little spark. We forgot them — and that's fixable."

**Key beats**

1. **Old Marlow's boat.** He remembers he used to sail. Friends from Act I give a sail, a compass and a rudder. The *Biscuit* carries you to the Silver Shallows, islands nobody remembers.
2. **Remembrance Hill.** The Hall of Names has one plaque per crystal, and many blank ones. Posy leaves flowers for names nobody claims.
3. **Eldergrove.** Ancient ring-trees. The Ringkeeper guards the Memoria Key.
4. **Foglight Marsh.** You walk Posy to the Nameless Stone, where she leaves a daisy "for a name nobody remembers".
5. **The Sunken Archive.** A great library half under the sea. The Hollow Fiend eats the long-ago. Beating it restores the Crystal of Memory, and a voice whispers one word: **"…Aster…"**
6. **Ember can fly.** A new spell, *Remembrance*, holds you up when you're about to fall. Wisp, a fairy made of unanswered questions, says, "Something is remembering ME."

### Act III — The Starfall Sea

**Story message:** keep telling your stories out loud. People who share them never forget.

**The setup.** Ember flies you over the Great Fogbank to **Taleshore**, a land the fog never reached because its people kept telling their stories. They remember Umbra's real name, but it's rude to say a name its owner lost. You have to earn it.

**Key beats**

1. **Port Lantern**, a harbor town with a quest board.
2. **Two Chorus Keys** from two wardens: the Compass Golem (maps and places) and the Riddle Leviathan, which speaks only in riddles.
3. **Story places:** riddle stones on the Story Reef, songstones rung in order on Songstone Cliffs, a hushed Quiet Quarter, and a pen-pal letter carried home to Lumina's librarian.
4. **Chorus Isle.** The Hush Fiend "shushed the sea". Beating it restores the Crystal of Voices and everyone sings out loud. Instead of an omen, a small voice: **"She used to sing to us. The first sage. Before you forgot her."**
5. **Ember turns radiant** (gold, sparkling).
6. **The Blank Chart.** The Chartmaker has mapped all of Lumina except one empty space, right under the Crystal Spire.

### Act IV — The Name

**Story message:** remembering someone is the kindest power there is.

**The twist.** Umbra was never a monster. It was **Aster, Lumina's first Sage**, who collected every question nobody could answer. The world got tired of hard questions, stopped asking, and forgot her name. Umbra is what was left.

**Key beats**

1. **The door under the Spire.** Keeper Aurora: "The one place I never dared to look." Stairs lead down into the Dream Root, where Lumina keeps everything it forgot.
2. **Say Her Name.** Wisp asks for help. Five people each remember a piece of the first Sage: Grandmother Wick, Keeper Mnem, the Chartmaker, Sage Chronicle, and Young Wick. The name is **Aster**. Wisp lights up with a new face: "I was part of her — the part that loved asking why."
3. **Lumina-That-Was.** A golden-hour memory of the old Field, the old Village and the First Classroom. You meet young Wick and young Lumen, and an Unnamed Dog that you get to name.
4. **Aster's Garden and the Nameless Hall.** Rows of blank plaques, one with a daisy carved on it.
5. **The descent.** Seven floors down the Deep Root. Umbra's voice goes from booming to tired to a whisper: "I made the whole world forget her. I had to forget her too."
6. **The finale isn't a fight to zero.** Five questions, then Umbra asks the only question it has left: *"What was my name?"* You answer "Aster". The shadow loosens into little lights. A woman with a daisy behind her ear says, "I waited so long for someone to remember me. And you did."
7. **After the credits.** Aster teaches again in the First Classroom. Her name goes on the first plaque on Remembrance Hill, and the Hall of Names has no blank plaques left.
8. **Epilogue.** Every named person in the world has a new line once her name is said, so the whole of Lumina remembers.

## Early hooks: the first 15 minutes

The opening gives a child something to care for (the egg), a hero that's theirs, and a win within minutes, all before any reading-heavy story.

| Hook | What the child sees | Why it lands for young kids |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Fun first, story second | Short quiz rounds before any cutscene | Kids play before they're asked to read. Passing feels like earning their way in. |
| Pick your hero | Five animal heroes, each with two special abilities | Choosing is ownership. Animals read instantly, even at age 6. |
| The last dragon egg | An egg that bobs behind you, "warm as a good idea" | Something small to protect. Kids want to know what's inside. |
| Ember hatches on the first win | A short cutscene. The baby dragon follows you and sneezes sparks. | A big emotional payoff early. It ties winning to the dragon growing. |
| Answers are the attacks | Every swing, spell and block is a question | A right answer instantly *does* something. No homework feeling. |
| No game over | A loss wakes you at the inn, healed. Mistakes show the right answer kindly. | A safe place to try. Wrong answers never punish. |
| Funny, warm villagers | One joke each ("VERY uncountable sheep") | Laughter keeps reluctant readers clicking. |
| A clear next step | A flag on the map and "Where to next?" from guides | Young players never feel lost. |
| Pitched to their age | Questions start at the child's age and adjust as they play | Early wins come fast without being babyish. |

## What keeps them playing through every act

Each act adds a new way to travel, a mystery to follow, and stronger friends. So every hour of play promises something new, and the story's questions draw kids on.

**1. Ember grows with the child.** Egg, then hatchling at the first win, whelp at 2 crystals, dragon at 4, a flying mount at 5, and radiant gold at 6. Each stage brings a bigger fire spell. It's the clearest sign of progress in the game.

**2. The world gets bigger as they do.** First on foot, then by Marlow's boat (Act II), then flying on Ember (Act III), then deep underground (Act IV). Fog banks lift on screen after each crystal, showing what's newly open.

**3. A mystery that pays off slowly.** Omens on the Spire after each crystal (Act I), a whisper of "Aster" (Act II), "she used to sing to us" (Act III), then the name itself (Act IV). Older kids put it together. Younger kids feel it.

**4. Friends who join the fight.** Pip and Wisp join when their quests are done, with Pair Attacks alongside the hero. Wisp's own story (who it really is) runs all the way to the finale.

**5. Collecting things.** Spells from Sages and shrines, badges from shops and bosses, warden keys, hidden secrets (a faint ✦ sparkle), and quests of many kinds: fetch, escort, delivery, riddle chains, songs.

**6. Challenge that keeps up.** Five quick right answers in a row make the questions harder mid-battle. After two losses to the same critter, the questions ease off. Far-off places have tougher critters, marked with "!" so kids can choose.

**7. Coming-back loops.** A daily streak, levels and power-up choices, and the Library, which brings back missed questions to try again.

**8. Payoffs for remembering.** Act I people come back changed: Marlow sails, Wick's lost letter is finally delivered, and young Wick and young Lumen appear in the memory world. Kids who played early are rewarded for remembering, which is the story's own message.

**How engagement builds, act by act**

| Act | New way to play | Mystery thread | Emotional peak |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| I | Walking a big open world, crystals in any order | Who is the voice on the Spire? | Ember hatches, then beating Umbra |
| II | Sailing, island-hopping, an escort | "…Aster…" | Posy's flower, then Ember can fly |
| III | Flying, riddles and songs, two-key gates | "She used to sing to us" | Taleshore sings out loud again |
| IV | Memory world, a seven-floor descent | Who Umbra really was | Saying her name |

## Open questions to confirm

- [ ] **Aster's name and "she".** These were placeholder choices in `STORY-4X.md`, and they're now in the game. Keep them?
- [ ] **The tone of the twist.** The villain turns out to be a forgotten teacher. It's the saddest beat in the game and is written to land warm. Is this the message you want for ages 6–18?
- [ ] **The warm-up has no story.** Kids play a few quiz rounds before the egg and the opening panels. Keep it, or show a short hook (the egg) first?
- [ ] **New Game+.** The finale points to replaying with harder riddles, but New Game+ isn't planned in these acts. Keep the promise or soften it?
